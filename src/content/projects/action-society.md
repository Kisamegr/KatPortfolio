## The Brief

A change management strategy exploring how multiple actors in Skäggetorp could collaborate more effectively and build long-term shared ownership.

Client: Action Society  
Role: Project Manager & Research Lead  
Focus: Change Management · Social Sustainability · Collaboration  
Framework: Kotter’s 8-Step Change Model  
Outcome: Collaboration strategy + implementation roadmap

How can multiple actors move from collaboration to shared ownership in practice? What structures and processes support collaboration? What role do leadership and facilitation play? How do group dynamics influence collaboration between organizations?

## The Challenge

Multiple actors were already working toward social sustainability in Skäggetorp, but collaboration was fragmented. Communication gaps, siloed ways of working, and limited shared coordination made it difficult to build trust, shared ownership, and long-term preventive action.

## Research & Understanding the System

To understand why collaboration was difficult, we combined desktop research, interviews, stakeholder analysis, and existing platform research.

### Key Research Findings

**01 — Collaboration already exists**  
Actors were already working together, but communication and coordination remained fragmented.

**02 — Trust is essential**  
Long-term collaboration depends on trust, psychological safety, and strong relationships.

**03 — Technology isn’t enough**  
Digital tools can support collaboration, but they cannot replace communication, relationships, and shared ownership.

## Reframing the Challenge

We explored the deeper causes behind weak shared ownership to understand where change was actually needed.

### The Turning Point

**We thought:** Actors need to collaborate more.

**We discovered:** Actors already want to collaborate.

**The real challenge:** Creating sustainable collaboration through stronger coordination, communication, and shared ownership.

This shifted our focus from creating more collaboration to improving the systems and conditions that help existing collaboration last.

## From Insights to Strategy

Based on our research, we developed a strategy combining digital tools with human-centered collaboration.

### COLLABORATION hub

Dashboards & KPI tracking. Multilingual access. Shared calendar. Activity mapping. Social media integration. AI data visualization features. (A version for actors & for residents)

![Action Society collaboration hub](/portfolio-assets/project-story/action-society-collaboration-hub.png)

### COMMUNICATION PLATFORM

- Slack for daily messaging
- Different channels → different projects/groups
- Important updates, resources, or discussions can be shared
- Action Society as the facilitator

*(Only for actors)*

![Action Society communication platform](/portfolio-assets/project-story/action-society-communication-platform.png)

### Facebook, LinkedIn and Instagram

Visibility, storytelling, engagement.

**Facebook**

- Create official page
- Invite all project actors
- Share project updates weekly
- Create discussion posts

**LinkedIn**

- Publish one post per week
- Share project learnings
- Highlight partnerships

**Instagram**

- Redesign visual identity
- Create templates
- Post 2–3 times weekly

### WORKSHOPS

- Trust-building activities
- Shared problem-solving sessions
- Involving the actors in designing sessions → how can it benefit you?

## From Strategy to Change

To turn the strategy into a long-term change process, we used Kotter’s 8-Step Change Model and adapted it to Action Society’s context.

## Turning the Framework into Action

We translated Kotter’s model into a phased roadmap for long-term implementation.

**4-5 ACTIVATION**

- Onboard collaboration & communication hub
- Monthly trust-building workshops & shared problem-solving sessions
- Collab. visualization, framtidskartan/digital twin integration

6 months - 1 year

## Reflection

This project taught me that project management is not only about creating a plan — it is also about helping a team move forward when the direction is unclear. Navigating a complex brief affected our momentum at times, and taught me to create structure while staying flexible and keeping the team aligned through uncertainty. I also learned that sustainable change is deeply human. Tools and systems can support change, but long-term impact depends on trust, relationships, and shared ownership.
