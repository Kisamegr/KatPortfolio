## Brief

Designing and co-facilitating a 75-minute workshop exploring how psychological safety and communication styles can support better-functioning teams.

Role: Co-Facilitator  
Duration: 1h 15min workshop  
Participants: 6  
Course: Team Leadership  
Focus: Psychological Safety · Communication · Team Dynamics

## Challenge

Our challenge was to design and co-facilitate a 75-minute learning experience for six participants. We had only a few hours to combine our individual topics, design the process, prepare the material, and facilitate the session together. My topic was psychological safety, while my co-facilitator explored different communication styles in group decision-making.

We combined our topics around one central question: **How can psychological safety and awareness of different communication styles help groups work better together?** The challenge was not only to explain these concepts, but to create a space where participants could experience, reflect, and connect the learning to their own team projects.

## Approach

We designed the workshop as a journey from safety → self-awareness → communication → collaboration. We started with an interactive Never Have I Ever check-in before introducing psychological safety. Participants then completed a psychological-safety self-assessment and reflected on their previous team experiences. From there, we explored different communication styles — including direct vs. indirect, expressive vs. reserved, and fast vs. slower processors — followed by individual and small-group reflection. Finally, we brought both topics together through a collaborative activity and closed with reflection on what participants wanted to take into their next team project.

## My role - Co-facilitator

As co-facilitator, I focused particularly on the psychological-safety part of the experience. I contributed to:

- Combining our two topics into one workshop journey
- Structuring the session and timing
- Creating the IDOART (Intention, Desired Outcome, Agenda, Roles, Rules, Time)
- Designing and facilitating the opening activity
- Introducing psychological safety
- Guiding the self-assessment and reflection
- Supporting participants and adapting to the energy in the room
- Co-facilitating the final activity and reflection

My responsibility was not simply to present information, but to create the conditions for participants to participate, reflect, and feel heard.

## Result

We successfully facilitated the full 75-minute session, moving participants between individual reflection, pair/small-group conversations, and collaborative activities. Participants explored their own experiences of psychological safety and communication and left with practical reflections they could carry into future team projects. For us as facilitators, the process also became a real example of working under pressure, adapting quickly, and sharing responsibility.

## Reflection & Learning

This project changed how I think about facilitation. I learned that facilitating is not about having all the answers or controlling the conversation. It is about designing a process that gives people enough structure and safety to contribute. I also learned how important timing, clear instructions, active listening, and flexibility are. A process can look perfect on paper, but as a facilitator, you need to observe the room and adapt when necessary. This experience connects strongly to the kind of Project Coordinator / Producer I want to become: someone who creates structure, supports different people and working styles, and helps teams collaborate effectively.
